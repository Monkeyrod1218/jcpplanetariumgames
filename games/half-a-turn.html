You earned it: Rotation Ranger

For teachers and families

The big idea: Earth makes one complete rotation on its axis about every 24 hours. That rotation is what causes day and night and what makes the Sun appear to move across the sky. Twelve hours is half of 24, so 12 hours equals one-half turn.

The three misconceptions this game targets, taken straight from the wrong answer choices: that the Sun travels around Earth; that the Sun stops shining at night; and that Earth's trip around the Sun (about 365 days) is the same motion as its daily spin.

One simplification: the model shows sunrise at 6:00 A.M. and sunset at 6:00 P.M., which is true near the equinoxes. Real sunrise and sunset times in Austin shift through the year because Earth's axis is tilted. Third graders don't need the tilt yet, but if a student notices, that's a great question to praise.
