Sun & Shadows(El Sol y las sombras)

Move the Sun across the sky and watch the shadow change.(Mueve el Sol por el cielo y observa cómo cambia la sombra.)

Morning(Mañana) Evening(Tarde)
As Earth spins, the Sun seems to cross the sky from east to west. That gives us day and night — and shadows that change all day. (Al girar la Tierra, el Sol parece cruzar el cielo de este a oeste. Eso nos da el día y la noche, y sombras que cambian todo el día.)

Now answer(Ahora responde)

☆☆

For teachers and families

The big idea: Earth rotates on its axis about once every 24 hours. That daily spin makes the Sun appear to move across the sky from east to west, which gives us day and night and makes shadows change length and direction through the day (answer D).

The distractors target common misconceptions: a full moon each month comes from the Moon's orbit (~29.5 days), not Earth's spin; the Sun appears to move east to west (not west to east), and it is really Earth turning; ocean tides are caused by the Moon's (and Sun's) gravity and occur day and night, not by rotation.

One simplification: the shadow model shows the Sun rising due east and setting due west with a symmetric arc, which is closest to true near the equinoxes.
