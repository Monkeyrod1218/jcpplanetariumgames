Long Days, Short Days(Días largos, días cortos)

Two cities. One has long days, one has short. Find out the season.(Dos ciudades. Una tiene días largos y otra cortos. Descubre la estación.)

1. How long is each day?(¿Cuánto dura cada día?)

Chicago
≈ 9 hrs
Sun up 7:18 a.m. → down 4:30 p.m. short day(El Sol sale 7:18 a. m. → se pone 4:30 p. m. · día corto)
Sydney
≈ 14 hrs
Sun up 5:47 a.m. → down 8:09 p.m. long day(El Sol sale 5:47 a. m. → se pone 8:09 p. m. · día largo)

2. Why? Tilt the Earth.(¿Por qué? Inclina la Tierra.)

Tilted toward the Sun = long days = summer. Tilted away = short days = winter. (Inclinado hacia el Sol = días largos = verano. Inclinado lejos = días cortos = invierno.)

Now answer(Ahora responde)

☆☆☆

For teachers and families

The big idea: Earth's axis is tilted about 23.5°. The hemisphere tilted toward the Sun has longer days and summer; the hemisphere tilted away has shorter days and winter. In the data, Sydney (Southern Hemisphere) has about 14 hours of daylight, so it is summer there; Chicago (Northern Hemisphere) has about 9 hours, so it is winter. The evidence for a long day is an early sunrise (5:47 a.m.) plus a late sunset (8:09 p.m.). The two hemispheres always have opposite seasons.

One simplification: the model treats tilt as the cause and keeps the two hemispheres symmetric. Real day lengths also depend on latitude, and these readings are near a solstice, when the difference is largest.
